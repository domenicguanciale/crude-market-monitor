Crude Market Monitor

When is the oil market tight, and how do prices react when supply is disrupted while it is tight? A student research project that tracks US inventories, global prices, shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, and geopolitical risk, and replays the 2026 disruption on one time axis.

Research and monitoring tool, not trading or betting advice

This week's reading

The score compares US crude stocks, distillate stocks and refinery utilization with the same week in the prior five years (2020 left out). It uses US data only, which is why the Brent minus WTI gap sits beside it: a wide gap means the world is short while the US is comparatively well supplied.

How one choice changes the score

The five-year comparison can keep 2020, when the pandemic swelled stocks and idled refineries, or leave it out. Leaving it out is the default here, decided before the backtest was run.

Does a tight week predict prices? No.

Each score is dated to the Wednesday it was published, then compared with the price change over the next four weeks (2010 to 2026). After tight weeks, prices did no better than after an average week. The rises after loose weeks come mostly from a few recoveries, above all 2020. The score describes the physical market; it is not a forecast.

Method and limits

  • Score: each input's position in its five-year range for the same week (0 at the low, 1 at the high). Stocks score +1 below 0.25 and −1 above 0.75; utilization the other way round. Tight at +2 or more, loose at −2 or less.
  • Price is not an input, so "tight markets have high prices" is never true by construction.
  • US data only. The score measures US conditions; the spread and shipping data are the global view.
  • Events appear only after they have been checked by hand against their sources. The 2026 war is treated as one episode with overlapping sub-events.
  • Shipping counts come from AIS signals; ships that switch transponders off are missed.
  • Weekly figures are estimates and are sometimes revised. Nothing here forecasts disruptions or prices.
