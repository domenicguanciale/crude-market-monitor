Crude Market Monitor in 3D

Three views of the same research, on one time axis. The globe shows tanker traffic through six shipping chokepoints. The skyline shows thirty years of the weekly US tightness score. How to read this

  • Shows what moved together, not what caused what. Tanker counts, prices and the score come from different sources and do not prove one another.
  • Tanker pillars. Height is the 7-day average of tankers per day. The ring on each pillar marks that chokepoint's own median for 2019 to 2025, so a pillar below its ring is running under its usual traffic. Counts come from satellite ship signals and miss ships that switch them off.
  • Skyline. One bar per week. Columns run Jan to Dec, rows run 1996 (back) to 2026 (front). Bars above the floor mean the US market is tight against the same week in the prior five years, bars below it mean loose. Bars after the selected date are faded.
  • The 2020 switch. The default leaves 2020 out of every five-year range, because the pandemic swelled stocks and widens the range. Keep it in and 2021 to 2025 read tighter. Same data, different method.
  • US data only. The score ignores the rest of the world, which is why Brent minus WTI sits beside it. A wide gap with a "normal" score is a global shortage the US data does not show.

Prices on weekends and holidays carry the last close forward. The weekly score is dated to the Friday that ends the week, and EIA publishes it the following Wednesday.
